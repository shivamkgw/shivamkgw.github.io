Who's Facing the Most Criminal Cases in the Lok Sabha?

Every sitting Lok Sabha MP with a digitized criminal-case entry on their election affidavit, mapped by state. Every figure below links back to its source.

Shivam Kumar | Loading dataset…
Read this before the map: these are cases filed against an MP in their sworn ECI affidavit, not convictions. Under Indian law, an accused is presumed innocent until proven guilty. Data reflects self-declared pending cases in sworn ECI affidavits (Form 26) at nomination time; cases filed after the election, or never declared by the candidate, fall outside this scope. This covers 484 of 543 winning candidates: the rest did not have a digitized criminal-case entry in the source list at the time of collection, so they are left out rather than guessed at. Zero declared cases is shown as a plain fact, not a "good" score. The map colors each state by its true average across every MP counted there, since this is now a full count of that state's MPs, not a small sample, so an average is statistically sound. Source for every number: MyNeta / ADR, based on public ECI election affidavits. The first 18 rows (the highest case counts) were individually cross-checked against their affidavit pages; the rest are sourced directly from MyNeta's published list, each linked to its own affidavit page for independent verification. Data collected: 27 July 2026. MyNeta records can change after court dispositions, so treat this as a point-in-time snapshot, not a live feed. This dataset stays largely static until the next full refresh: the 18th Lok Sabha's term runs to around mid-2029, when the next general election forces every MP to file a fresh affidavit. Between now and then, expect only occasional single-seat updates from bye-elections; state assembly election data does not apply to this page, since it tracks Lok Sabha (national) MPs only.

Declared assets against declared criminal cases

Declared assets often reflect book or purchase-price value at the time of filing, not current market value, especially for long-held agricultural or urban land. Treat this axis as a floor on true wealth, not an exact figure. Assets axis uses a logarithmic scale since values span from a few lakh to hundreds of crore; a linear scale would compress almost every MP into one corner. Dots with 0 or few cases are jittered slightly apart so overlapping MPs stay visible rather than merging into one line; the dashed lines mark the median wealth (≈₹7.2 Cr) and the national average case count, dividing the chart into four quadrants.

A Different Dataset · MPs and MLAs, All India

Crimes Against Women: Sitting MPs and MLAs

This is not the Lok Sabha dataset above. Everything on this page so far covers 484 sitting Lok Sabha MPs and general declared criminal cases. This section is a separate report from ADR / National Election Watch, published 21 August 2024, covering a specific serious-crime category (crimes against women, including rape) across a wider population: 755 sitting MPs (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha) and 3,938 sitting MLAs nationwide. The two datasets are not directly comparable: different scope, different source report, different population size. Of 151 lawmakers with declared cases in this category, 135 are MLAs and 16 are MPs; 16 have declared cases specifically under rape charges (IPC 376). These are cases filed, not convictions; under Indian law, an accused is presumed innocent until proven guilty. Every figure here was independently verified against ADR's own published PDF report, not taken from a secondary source.
